<p>The smell of lemon and fresh herbs hitting hot grill grates is the kind of promise that makes a meal feel like an occasion. But I've ruined my share of salmon — dry, chalky, stuck to the grates. That's why I developed this method. It's not about a secret ingredient. It's about a strict ratio and a timing trick that gives you consistently flaky, moist fish with that beautiful char.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/151570b8-2efe-4c0d-8061-ae6a19402357/0_2.png" alt="Salmon fillets marinating in a glass dish with lemon herb mixture" /></figure>


<h2>Why This Ratio Matters</h2>
<p>The marinade is where most people go wrong. Too much acid and the fish turns mushy. Too little and the flavor is flat. I keep the ratio at <strong>1 part lemon juice to 1 part olive oil</strong> — exactly 1/4 cup each for four fillets. The oil carries the herb flavor and protects the fish from drying out. The lemon adds brightness without overpowering. And here's the crucial detail: <strong>never marinate longer than 15 minutes</strong>. Set a timer. The acid in the lemon cooks the proteins just like heat does. Beyond 15 minutes, you're essentially starting to ceviche the outside of the salmon, and it will fall apart on the grill.</p>

<h2>The Grilling Technique: Indirect Heat, Direct Results</h2>
<p>Most people blast salmon directly over high heat and wonder why it's dry. Here's the method I use: preheat your grill to medium-high (about 400°F). Clean the grates thoroughly — a wire brush and a quick oil with a paper towel held in tongs is non-negotiable. Then, set up for two-zone grilling: turn one side of the burners to high, the other side to low. Place the salmon skin-side down on the hot side for exactly 2 minutes to get those beautiful grill marks. Then, slide the fillets to the cooler side, close the lid, and let them cook <strong>without flipping</strong> for 6 minutes. This indirect heat gently cooks the fish through without burning the outside. The skin acts as a natural barrier, protecting the delicate flesh.</p>

<h3>The Flip and The Baste</h3>
<p>After 6 minutes, use a thin metal spatula to gently flip the salmon. Don't try to lift it with tongs — you'll break the fillet. The skin should release easily if the grates were clean and oiled. If it sticks, give it another 30 seconds. Once flipped, brush the reserved marinade (the 2 tablespoons you set aside earlier) onto the cooked side. Close the lid and cook for another 4-6 minutes. This is where the instant-read thermometer earns its keep. Insert it into the thickest part of the fillet. You're aiming for <strong>125°F for medium</strong> (my preference — it stays silky and moist) or 130°F for well-done. Remember, the fish will carry-over cook another 5°F while resting.</p>

<h2>Visual Cues: What to Look For</h2>
<p>Numbers are great, but your eyes matter too. The salmon is ready when the flesh turns from translucent to opaque all the way through. The thickest part should still have a slight rosy center if you're going for medium. The edges will look flaky and the surface will feel firm but springy when pressed gently. If you see white albumin — those white curds on the surface — you've cooked it a touch too long. Still edible, but next time pull it off a minute earlier.</p>

<h3>The Common Mistake to Avoid</h3>
<p>Don't move the fish around once it's on the hot side. Let it sit for the full 2 minutes. Moving it too early tears the skin and makes it stick. Also, resist the temptation to press down on the fillets with a spatula. That squeezes out the moisture and leaves you with dry fish. Let the grill do the work.</p>

<h2>Serving and Storing</h2>
<p>Let the salmon rest for 2 minutes off the grill before serving. This allows the juices to redistribute. Serve with lemon wedges and a sprinkle of fresh dill or parsley. Leftovers? Store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 2 days. Reheat gently in a 300°F oven for 5-7 minutes — never the microwave, which turns it to rubber. Flake it cold into salads or grain bowls for an easy lunch.</p>

<h2>Takeaway</h2>
<p>Grilled salmon doesn't have to be a gamble. Stick to the 15-minute marinade limit, use indirect heat for most of the cooking, and trust your thermometer. You'll get consistently flaky, bright, herb-kissed fish that tastes like summer on a plate.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>How long should I marinate salmon before grilling?</h3>
<p>No more than 15 minutes. Because the marinade contains lemon juice (acid), longer marinating will begin to break down the proteins and make the fish mushy or 'cooked' on the outside before it hits the grill.</p>
<h3>What temperature should grilled salmon be?</h3>
<p>For moist, flaky salmon, cook to an internal temperature of 125°F for medium, or 130°F for well-done. The temperature will rise about 5°F during resting. Use an instant-read thermometer for accuracy.</p>
<h3>Should I grill salmon with the skin on or off?</h3>
<p>Skin on is best for grilling. The skin acts as a protective barrier against the high heat, preventing the delicate flesh from drying out or sticking. It also makes flipping easier. You can remove it after cooking if you prefer.</p>
<h3>How do I keep salmon from sticking to the grill?</h3>
<p>Clean your grill grates thoroughly with a wire brush, then oil them well before preheating. Use a paper towel dipped in vegetable oil and held with tongs to rub the grates. Also, make sure the salmon skin is dry before placing it on the grill.</p>
