<p>The heat of summer clings to the kitchen even after sunset. I know the feeling: you want a pizza night that feels special but doesn't turn your house into a sauna. The secret isn't just the pizza—it's what you drink alongside it.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/0010c214-ad9a-4622-ad31-98bd319f5dd0/0_2.png" alt="Pouring cold brew concentrate from a French press into a glass filled with ice" /></figure>
<p>Most people grab a soda or a beer. But I've found that a cold brew float, made with a precise 1:4 concentrate, cuts through the richness of melted cheese and tangy tomato sauce in a way that no other drink can. The bitterness of the coffee meets the salt of the pepperoni, and the vanilla ice cream ties it all together.</p><h2>Why Cold Brew Works for Pizza Night</h2><p>Hot coffee with pizza? Too much heat. Regular iced coffee? Often too acidic and watery. Cold brew concentrate, on the other hand, is smooth, low in acid, and intensely flavorful. The long steep—18 hours at room temperature—extracts the sugars and oils from the coffee grounds without pulling out the bitter compounds that high heat releases. The result is a coffee that can stand up to the bold flavors of pizza without overpowering them.</p><h3>The Ratio That Makes the Difference</h3><p>I keep the ratio at 1 part coffee to 4 parts water by weight. For a French press, that means 1 cup (about 85 grams) of coarse-ground coffee to 4 cups (960 ml) of cold, filtered water. The grind should feel like coarse sea salt—not powdery, not pebbly. If you grind too fine, you'll get a muddy, over-extracted concentrate. Too coarse, and the brew will be weak and watery.</p><p>After 18 hours, press the plunger down slowly. The liquid should be dark as molasses and smell intensely of chocolate and caramel. If it smells sour, you either over-steeped or the grind was too fine. That's the common mistake: leaving it longer than 20 hours. Stick to 18.</p><h2>Building the Perfect Float</h2><p>You don't need a fancy setup. Two tall glasses, a scoop of vanilla ice cream, and your cold brew concentrate. But the order matters.</p><ol><li>Fill your glasses with ice first. This chills the glass and prevents the ice cream from melting too fast.</li><li>Pour 2 ounces of cold brew concentrate over the ice.</li><li>Add 4 ounces of cold water. Stir gently with a long spoon—just enough to combine, not to melt the ice.</li><li>Top with one scoop of vanilla ice cream. Let it sit for 30 seconds before serving, so the ice cream begins to soften and create a creamy foam layer.</li><li>Finish with a tiny pinch of flaky sea salt. This isn't just for show—the salt amplifies the coffee's sweetness and cuts through the pizza's greasy richness.</li></ol><p>The ice cream should be high-quality, with real vanilla specks. Cheap ice cream uses artificial flavors that clash with the coffee's natural notes. If you're dairy-free, coconut milk ice cream works surprisingly well—its tropical sweetness complements the coffee's chocolate undertones.</p><h2>Timing Your Pizza and Coffee</h2><p>Here's the trick most people miss: the cold brew needs to be ready before you start the pizza. I make my concentrate the night before, so by 6 PM the next day, it's perfectly steeped and chilled. If you're making pizza from scratch, start the dough 2 hours before you plan to eat. The cold brew can sit in the fridge while you stretch the dough and preheat the oven.</p><p>When the pizza comes out of the oven, let it rest for 2 minutes. That's when you assemble the floats. The contrast between the hot, crispy crust and the cold, creamy coffee is the whole point. Serve the float alongside the pizza, not after. Sip between bites—the coffee cleanses your palate, making each bite of pizza taste as good as the first.</p><h2>A Note on Caffeine</h2><p>Caffeine affects everyone differently. Some people can drink this float at 8 PM and sleep soundly; others will be staring at the ceiling at midnight. If you're sensitive, use decaf beans for the cold brew concentrate. The flavor profile remains similar—decaf cold brew can be just as smooth and chocolatey—without the stimulant effect. For anyone with specific health concerns, including pregnancy or a medical condition, it's best to consult a qualified professional. Coffee and caffeine are not suitable for young children.</p><h2>The Takeaway</h2><p>Summer pizza night doesn't have to be complicated. The key is preparation: make your cold brew concentrate a day ahead, keep the ratio tight, and don't skip the salt. The result is a meal that feels like a treat without pushing your kitchen past its limits. One sip, one bite, and you'll wonder why you ever paired pizza with anything else.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Can I use regular brewed coffee instead of cold brew?</h3>
<p>Yes, but it will be more acidic and less smooth. Cold brew's low acidity is what makes it pair so well with tomato-based pizza sauce. If using hot-brewed coffee, chill it completely first and use double the amount since it's less concentrated.</p>
<h3>What type of pizza goes best with this cold brew float?</h3>
<p>A classic pepperoni or Margherita pizza works great. The saltiness of pepperoni and the fresh basil in Margherita balance the creamy, slightly bitter coffee float. Avoid overly sweet or spicy pizzas, which can clash with the coffee.</p>
<h3>How long does cold brew concentrate last?</h3>
<p>Stored in an airtight container in the refrigerator, cold brew concentrate stays fresh for up to two weeks. After that, it starts to lose its bright flavor and can turn bitter.</p>
