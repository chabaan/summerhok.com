<p>The sizzle of butter hitting a hot pan. The sharp, clean smell of lemon zest. Garlic softening in white wine until the whole kitchen smells like a seaside trattoria. That is the promise of one-pan summer shrimp scampi — and it delivers in under 20 minutes, with no oven, no grill, and just one skillet to wash.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/8a104ffc-cd91-4d69-8847-35f23e5068b8/0_3.png" alt="Shrimp cooking in a skillet with garlic, white wine, and lemon butter sauce" /></figure>

<p>This recipe is built for a hot evening when you want something bright, fast, and satisfying. The technique hinges on two things: cooking the shrimp just until pink (they finish in the sauce), and building a sauce that clings to every strand of pasta. Here is how to do it right.</p>

<h2>Choose the Right Shrimp</h2>
<p>Look for large shrimp, 21 to 25 per pound (often labeled "21-25 count"). They are big enough to stay juicy through the quick cook time but small enough to eat in a single bite with pasta. Peel and devein them yourself — it takes five minutes and saves you from the rubbery texture of pre-cooked frozen shrimp. Pat them dry with paper towels before seasoning. Wet shrimp will steam instead of sear, and you will lose that golden edge that adds flavor to the sauce.</p>

<h2>Build the Sauce in Layers</h2>
<p>The order matters here. Start by searing the shrimp in a mixture of butter and olive oil. The oil raises the smoke point so the butter does not burn. Cook the shrimp in a single layer — if they crowd, they steam. Flip once after one minute, cook another minute, then transfer to a plate. They will not be fully cooked through; that is fine. They will finish in the sauce.</p>
<p>Now the garlic. Slice it thinly rather than mincing — thin slices infuse the oil without burning as easily. Cook it just until fragrant and pale gold, about 30 seconds. Burnt garlic turns acrid and ruins the sauce. Add the red pepper flakes at this stage if you like a gentle warmth.</p>
<p>Deglaze with white wine and chicken broth. Scrape up any browned bits from the bottom of the pan — that is concentrated shrimp flavor. Let the liquid simmer until it reduces by half, about 3 to 4 minutes. The sauce should look glossy and coat the back of a spoon. Stir in lemon zest and lemon juice at the end, after reducing, because heat dulls the brightness of lemon juice over time.</p>

<h2>Pasta: Cook It Right</h2>
<p>Cook your pasta one minute less than the package directs for al dente. It will finish cooking in the sauce and absorb some of that garlic-lemon liquid. Reserve a cup of pasta water before draining — the starch in that water helps emulsify the sauce so it clings to the pasta instead of pooling in the bottom of the bowl. Add it a tablespoon at a time until the sauce looks silky, not watery.</p>

<h2>Bring It All Together</h2>
<p>Toss the drained pasta with the sauce, then add the shrimp back in along with any juices that have collected on the plate. Toss for 30 seconds — just enough to warm the shrimp through without overcooking them. Finish with a shower of fresh parsley. The parsley adds a clean, grassy note that cuts through the butter and garlic.</p>
<p>Serve immediately. This dish waits for no one. Have everything ready before you start cooking: pasta drained, shrimp prepped, lemon juiced, parsley chopped. The active cook time is about 10 minutes, and it goes fast.</p>

<h2>One-Pan Summer Shrimp Scampi</h2>
<p><strong>Yield:</strong> 4 servings<br>
<strong>Prep time:</strong> 10 minutes | <strong>Cook time:</strong> 10 minutes</p>

<h3>Ingredients</h3>
<ul>
<li>1 1/2 pounds large shrimp (21-25 count), peeled and deveined</li>
<li>3 tablespoons unsalted butter, divided</li>
<li>2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil</li>
<li>4 cloves garlic, thinly sliced</li>
<li>1/4 teaspoon red pepper flakes (optional)</li>
<li>1/2 cup dry white wine (such as Pinot Grigio or Sauvignon Blanc)</li>
<li>1/2 cup low-sodium chicken broth</li>
<li>Zest of 1 lemon</li>
<li>3 tablespoons fresh lemon juice (from about 1 lemon)</li>
<li>1/4 cup finely chopped fresh parsley</li>
<li>1/2 teaspoon kosher salt, plus more for shrimp</li>
<li>1/4 teaspoon black pepper</li>
<li>8 ounces dried linguine or angel hair pasta</li>
<li>1/4 cup reserved pasta water (if needed)</li>
</ul>

<h3>Instructions</h3>
<ol>
<li>Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Cook pasta 1 minute less than package directions for al dente. Reserve 1/4 cup pasta water, then drain pasta. Set aside.</li>
<li>While pasta cooks, pat shrimp dry with paper towels. Season lightly with salt and pepper.</li>
<li>Heat a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add 1 tablespoon butter and the olive oil. When butter foams, add shrimp in a single layer. Cook without moving for 1 minute, then flip and cook 1 minute more until just pink and opaque. Transfer shrimp to a plate (they will finish cooking in the sauce).</li>
<li>Reduce heat to medium. Add remaining 2 tablespoons butter, garlic, and red pepper flakes. Cook, stirring constantly, until garlic is fragrant and just golden, about 30 seconds. Do not brown the garlic — it turns bitter.</li>
<li>Pour in white wine and chicken broth. Increase heat to medium-high and bring to a simmer. Cook, scraping up any browned bits from the pan, until liquid reduces by about half, 3 to 4 minutes.</li>
<li>Stir in lemon zest, lemon juice, and the cooked pasta. Toss to coat. If the sauce seems too thick, add reserved pasta water a tablespoon at a time until it glistens.</li>
<li>Return shrimp to the skillet, along with any accumulated juices. Toss for 30 seconds just to warm through. Remove from heat.</li>
<li>Sprinkle with fresh parsley and serve immediately. Pass extra lemon wedges at the table.</li>
</ol>

<h3>Notes</h3>
<p><strong>Storage:</strong> Best eaten fresh. Leftovers keep in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 1 day. Reheat gently in a skillet with a splash of broth to revive the sauce. Not suitable for freezing (shrimp turns rubbery).</p>
<p><strong>Serving tip:</strong> Serve with crusty bread to soak up every drop of the garlic-lemon butter. For a lighter meal, swap pasta for zucchini noodles — add them raw in step 6 and toss just until warmed.</p>
<p><strong>Allergen note:</strong> Contains shellfish and dairy. Gluten-free if using GF pasta. For dairy-free, use all olive oil and a splash of unsweetened oat milk or extra broth. For specific dietary concerns, consult a qualified professional.</p>

<h2>Takeaway</h2>
<p>One-pan summer shrimp scampi is exactly the kind of dinner you want on a hot night: fast, bright, and clean. The key is respecting the shrimp — cook it quickly and gently — and building a sauce that balances richness with acidity. Master those two things, and you have a go-to meal that feels special but takes almost no effort.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Can I use frozen shrimp for this recipe?</h3>
<p>Yes. Thaw frozen shrimp overnight in the refrigerator or place them in a colander under cold running water for about 10 minutes. Pat them very dry before cooking — excess moisture will steam the shrimp instead of browning them, and the sauce will become watery.</p>
<h3>What is the best wine substitute for shrimp scampi?</h3>
<p>Use dry white wine like Pinot Grigio, Sauvignon Blanc, or a unoaked Chardonnay. For a non-alcoholic option, replace the wine with an equal amount of low-sodium chicken broth plus 1 tablespoon of white wine vinegar or lemon juice to mimic the acidity.</p>
<h3>How do I know when the shrimp are perfectly cooked?</h3>
<p>Shrimp cook quickly. They are done when they turn pink and opaque throughout, and the tail meat curls into a loose 'C' shape. If they curl into a tight 'O', they are overcooked and will be tough. The internal temperature should reach 120°F (49°C) at the thickest part — they will continue cooking slightly off heat.</p>
