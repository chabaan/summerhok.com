<p>There's something about surf and turf that feels like a special occasion. But it doesn't have to be complicated. With just five ingredients — steak, shrimp, olive oil, salt, and pepper — you can put together a meal that tastes like it took hours. The trick is all in the heat and the timing.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/030cd8cb-4adc-42ad-87bb-648bbb79dac0/0_1.png" alt="Grilling shrimp and steak on a hot grill grate" /></figure>


<h2>Choose Your Ingredients Well</h2>
<p>Start with good steak. Ribeye or New York strip, about one inch thick, will give you a nice crust and a tender center. For the shrimp, go for large ones — 16 to 20 count per pound. Peeled and deveined saves you prep time. The oil, salt, and pepper are your only seasonings, so make them count. Use a flaky kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper.</p>

<h2>The Prep That Matters</h2>
<p>Take the steaks out of the fridge 30 minutes before cooking. This lets them come closer to room temperature, which helps them cook evenly. Pat them dry with paper towels — moisture is the enemy of a good sear. In a small bowl, mix the olive oil, salt, and pepper. Brush half of this mixture all over the steaks. Toss the shrimp in the remaining oil mixture.</p>
<p>This is the only marinade you need. The oil helps conduct heat, the salt draws out just enough moisture to create a crust, and the pepper adds a gentle warmth. No need for garlic or herbs here — the purity of the ingredients shines through.</p>

<h2>Fire Up the Grill</h2>
<p>Get your grill hot. High heat, around 450-500°F. Clean the grates well and oil them to prevent sticking. You want a clean, hot cooking surface. Place the steaks on the hottest part of the grill. Let them cook for 4 minutes without moving them. That first contact builds the crust. Flip and cook another 3 to 4 minutes for medium-rare. An instant-read thermometer is your best friend here — 130-135°F in the center is your target. For medium, go to 140°F.</p>
<p>In the last two minutes of the steak cooking time, add the shrimp. They cook fast. One to two minutes per side is all they need. Look for them to turn pink and opaque, and curl into a loose 'C' shape. If they curl into a tight 'O', they are overcooked.</p>

<h2>Rest and Serve</h2>
<p>Once the steaks come off the grill, let them rest for 5 minutes. This allows the juices to redistribute throughout the meat. If you cut into them right away, those juices end up on the plate instead of in your mouth. The shrimp can be served immediately. Plate the steak and shrimp together, and you're done.</p>

<h3>A Note on Caffeine and Grilling</h3>

<h2>Takeaway</h2>
<p>This 5-ingredient grilled surf and turf proves that simple cooking can be impressive. The key is a hot grill, dry meat, and careful timing. No complicated marinades, no fuss. Just good ingredients and a little patience. That's all you need for a meal that feels like a celebration.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>What is the best cut of steak for surf and turf?</h3>
<p>Ribeye or New York strip are great choices for their flavor and tenderness. Look for steaks about 1 inch thick for even grilling.</p>
<h3>Can I use frozen shrimp for this recipe?</h3>
<p>Yes, but thaw them completely in the refrigerator overnight or under cold running water. Pat them very dry before oiling and seasoning to avoid steaming on the grill.</p>
<h3>How do I know when the shrimp are done?</h3>
<p>Shrimp cook quickly. They are done when they turn pink and opaque, and curl into a loose 'C' shape. Overcooked shrimp become tough and rubbery.</p>
<h3>Do I need to marinate the steak or shrimp?</h3>
<p>No, this recipe keeps it simple with just oil, salt, and pepper. The high heat creates a nice crust. If you want extra flavor, you can add a sprinkle of garlic powder or smoked paprika.</p>
