<p>The first time I made this salad, I was struck by how a few simple ingredients could create something so satisfying. It was a hot July afternoon, and I wanted something light but still substantial enough to be a side dish for grilled chicken. The combination of sweet, juicy strawberries, creamy goat cheese, and crunchy toasted pecans over a bed of crisp spinach is a classic for good reason. The key is in the balance: the dressing should be tangy enough to cut through the richness of the cheese, but not so acidic that it overpowers the fruit.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/f33f8b34-bb5d-4920-96b8-ff69750758f1/0_3.png" alt="Close-up of a small jar with homemade poppy seed dressing being poured over the salad." /></figure>


<h2>The Secret to the Perfect Dressing Ratio</h2>
<p>Many recipes for poppy seed dressing are too sweet or too thick. I keep the ratio at 2 parts oil to 1 part acid, but I use apple cider vinegar instead of the more common white wine vinegar. The apple cider vinegar has a softer, fruitier acidity that complements the strawberries without clashing. The honey adds just enough sweetness to round out the tang. If you prefer a sharper dressing, increase the vinegar by a teaspoon. For a sweeter version, add an extra half tablespoon of honey. The poppy seeds are not just for looks; they add a subtle nuttiness and a slight crunch that ties everything together.</p>

<h3>How to Emulsify the Dressing</h3>
<p>Whisk the oil slowly into the vinegar and honey mixture, or shake vigorously in a jar with a tight lid. The dressing should look slightly opaque and thick, not separated. If it separates after sitting, just shake it again before using. This dressing will keep in the refrigerator for up to a week, but let it come to room temperature for a minute before whisking again, as the honey can thicken when cold.</p>

<h2>Choosing and Preparing the Strawberries</h2>
<p>Fresh, ripe strawberries are non-negotiable here. Look for berries that are deep red all the way through, with no white or green shoulders. The fragrance should be strong and sweet. One common mistake is slicing the berries too thin; they turn mushy and lose their texture. I slice each berry into three or four even slices, depending on size, so each bite has a substantial piece of fruit. If your strawberries are not at peak sweetness, you can macerate them in a teaspoon of sugar for 10 minutes before adding them to the salad. This draws out their juices and concentrates their flavor.</p>

<h2>Toasting Pecans for Maximum Crunch</h2>
<p>Toasting pecans is a small step that makes a huge difference. Spread the pecan halves in a single layer in a dry skillet over medium heat. Shake the pan every 30 seconds. You will know they are done when they become fragrant and the color deepens slightly. This takes about 3-4 minutes. Do not walk away; nuts burn quickly. Let them cool completely on a plate before adding to the salad, or they will wilt the spinach.</p>

<h2>Assembling the Salad</h2>
<p>Place the spinach in a large bowl. Add the sliced strawberries, crumbled goat cheese, and cooled pecans. Drizzle the dressing over the top, starting with about half. Gently toss with your hands or salad tongs, adding more dressing as needed until the leaves are lightly coated. You want every leaf to have a hint of dressing, but you do not want the salad swimming in it. Serve immediately. The spinach will start to wilt within minutes of being dressed, so this is not a salad to let sit.</p>

<h2>Why This Salad Works for Summer Gatherings</h2>
<p>This salad is a crowd-pleaser because it is familiar but elevated. The sweetness of the strawberries feels like summer, the tangy dressing wakes up your palate, and the goat cheese adds a creamy, tangy note that contrasts with the crunch of the pecans. It pairs beautifully with grilled meats, fish, or even a simple quiche. For a heartier meal, add grilled chicken or shrimp on top.</p>

<h2>Takeaway</h2>
<p>The best version of this salad comes down to the quality of your ingredients and the balance of your dressing. Use ripe strawberries, toast your nuts, and dress the salad just before serving. That is the whole secret. Enjoy it on a hot summer evening, and watch it disappear.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Can I use frozen strawberries for this salad?</h3>
<p>Fresh strawberries are best for texture and flavor. Frozen berries release too much water and will make the salad soggy.</p>
<h3>What can I substitute for goat cheese?</h3>
<p>Feta cheese, blue cheese crumbles, or shaved Parmesan all work well. For a dairy-free option, omit the cheese or use a plant-based crumble.</p>
<h3>How do I keep the spinach from getting soggy?</h3>
<p>Dry the spinach thoroughly after washing. A salad spinner works best. Also, toss the salad with dressing only right before serving.</p>
<h3>Can I make this salad ahead of time?</h3>
<p>You can prep the ingredients ahead: wash and dry spinach, slice strawberries, toast pecans, and make the dressing. But combine and dress the salad just before serving for the best texture.</p>
<h3>Is this salad suitable for kids?</h3>
<p>Yes, most kids enjoy the sweet strawberries and mild dressing. You can omit the pecans if nut allergies are a concern, or use sunflower seeds instead.</p>
