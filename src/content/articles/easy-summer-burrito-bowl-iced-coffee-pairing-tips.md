<p>The heat hits you the moment you step outside. It's the kind of day where turning on the stove feels like a punishment. You want something cold, something bright, something that doesn't ask you to hover over a hot pan. That's exactly where this easy summer burrito bowl comes in.</p>
<figure><img src="https://cdn.midjourney.com/110fb3a2-a4b3-48ca-acf8-1e7544a7a2cb/0_3.png" alt="Close-up of pouring cold brew concentrate into a glass of ice and water" /></figure>
<p>No cooking required. Just chopping, tossing, and a quick dressing that brings everything together. And because I'm a drinks developer at heart, I've paired it with a simple iced coffee that cuts through the richness of the avocado and the earthy black beans. The trick? A 1:4 ratio of cold brew concentrate to water. That's one part concentrate to four parts water. It gives you a smooth, low-acid coffee that doesn't overpower the meal.</p><h2>Why This Burrito Bowl Works for Summer</h2><p>Summer meals need to be quick and refreshing. This bowl hits both notes. The base is leftover rice—cooked ahead and chilled. No need to reheat it. Cold rice has a firmer texture that holds up to the dressing and the juicy tomatoes. The black beans add protein without any cooking. Just rinse them from the can, drain well, and they're ready.</p><p>The dressing is where the magic happens. Lime juice, olive oil, cumin, and chili powder. That's it. Whisk it together in a small bowl or shake it in a mason jar. The acidity from the lime wakes up every ingredient. The cumin adds warmth without heat. The chili powder gives a subtle kick.</p><p>I keep the ratio at 2 tablespoons lime juice to 2 tablespoons olive oil. That's a 1:1 ratio for the dressing base. It's bright but not sour, rich but not heavy. If you want more tang, add another teaspoon of lime juice. Taste as you go.</p><h2>The Iced Coffee Pairing</h2><p>Why iced coffee with a burrito bowl? The creaminess of the avocado and the starchiness of the rice need something to cut through them. A bright, clean iced coffee does exactly that. I use cold brew concentrate because it's less bitter than hot-brewed coffee that's been chilled. The cold extraction pulls out sweeter, smoother flavors.</p><p>To make the concentrate, use a coarse grind—think sea salt texture—and a 1:4 ratio of coffee to water by weight. Let it steep at room temperature for 12 to 18 hours. Then filter through a paper filter or a fine-mesh sieve. The result is a concentrate that keeps in the fridge for up to a week.</p><p>For serving, pour 2 tablespoons of concentrate over a glass full of ice. Top with 1 cup of cold water. Stir gently. The ice dilutes it just enough to bring the strength to a regular coffee level. If you prefer a stronger coffee, use 3 tablespoons of concentrate and reduce the water to 3/4 cup.</p><h2>Common Mistakes to Avoid</h2><p>One mistake I see often is using warm rice. Warm rice makes the avocado turn mushy and the dressing gets absorbed too quickly, leaving the bowl dry. Always use fully cooled rice. Cook it the night before and refrigerate it overnight.</p><p>Another mistake is not draining the black beans thoroughly. Excess liquid from the can thins out the dressing and makes the bowl watery. Rinse the beans under cold water, then let them sit in a colander for a minute or two.</p><p>For the iced coffee, the biggest mistake is using hot coffee straight over ice. It melts the ice too fast, giving you a watery drink. Always chill the coffee first, or use a concentrate that's already cold.</p><h2>How to Customize the Bowl</h2><p>This bowl is a template. Swap the rice for quinoa or cauliflower rice. Add grilled chicken or shrimp if you want more protein. Throw in some diced mango for sweetness. The dressing works with all of it.</p><p>If you're sensitive to caffeine, use decaf beans for the cold brew. The process is the same. Decaf cold brew still has that smooth, low-acid profile. Just remember that caffeine affects everyone differently. Start with a smaller serving if you're unsure.</p><p>One more tip: handle the knife carefully when dicing the avocado. The pit can be slippery. Cut around it, twist the halves apart, and scoop the flesh out with a spoon. Then dice on a cutting board.</p><h2>The Takeaway</h2><p>This easy summer burrito bowl isn't about fussy techniques or hard-to-find ingredients. It's about using what you have—cold rice, a can of beans, a ripe avocado—and letting a simple dressing and a well-made iced coffee elevate it. The 1:1 dressing ratio and the 1:4 coffee-to-water ratio are your anchors. Stick to those, and you'll have a meal that feels like a break from the heat, not a chore.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<h3>Can I use leftover rice for this burrito bowl?</h3>
<p>Absolutely. Day-old rice works perfectly because it's drier and won't get mushy. Just fluff it with a fork before adding.</p>
<h3>What if I don't have cold brew concentrate?</h3>
<p>You can use strong brewed coffee that's been chilled. Brew double-strength by using twice the coffee grounds you normally would, then cool it down.</p>
<h3>How do I keep the avocado from browning?</h3>
<p>Toss the diced avocado with a little extra lime juice before adding to the bowl. The acidity slows oxidation.</p>
